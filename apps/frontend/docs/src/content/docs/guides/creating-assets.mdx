title: Creating Your Own Assets
description: Use the in-game Creator Studio to generate a portrait, prop or scene with your local engine, keep it in your library, and have the game render it.
---

The Creator Studio is the player-facing half of local asset generation: pick an
asset type, write a prompt, generate with your local engine, review the result,
and save it. Everything happens on your device — no account, no upload.

## Open the studio

From the start menu, open **Advanced → Creator Studio** (or navigate straight to
`/studio/assets`). No campaign is required.

1. Pick an **asset type** (a recipe). Image recipes generate immediately. Music,
   ambience and one-shot recipes need the local **asset runner** — until its
   browser-facing front door ships the studio lists them with that reason rather
   than a bare disabled control (see **Audio assets** below).
2. Write a **prompt**. For a portrait or expression, also give the **NPC id** so
   the game can find the result in dialogue.
3. **Generate**, then review the result — tag, extension, engine and seed are
   shown so you can reproduce it. An audio candidate is reviewed with a decoded
   **waveform**, labelled **Play/Pause**, **Loop audition** and **Mute**
   controls, and a spoken status line.
4. **Save to library**. The bytes are written to your local asset cache and a
   registry row, and the asset resolves in-game through its tag.

Saved assets appear in **My library** with their provenance (`generated:<engine>`)
and size. You can rename or delete them; deleting also removes the cached bytes
and warns you when a save references the tag. Locally generated assets are never
evicted by the cache's LRU policy — they are your work, not catalog data.

## Audio assets

Music, ambience and sound effects go through a finishing stage that measures the
*decoded* bytes and keeps a lossless master plus a runtime rendition:

- A master is archived exactly as it arrived. Nothing is rewritten in place, and
the archival hash is the lineage anchor every rendition points back to.
- Music is delivered as 48 kHz stereo Opus in `.webm` at about **-18 LUFS**
integrated (±2) with true peak at or below **-1 dBTP**; ambience targets about
**-24 LUFS** (±3). Short one-shots are calibrated by peak/RMS instead, because a
gated loudness measurement needs a 400 ms window a one-shot does not have.
- A near-silent master is **refused**, never normalised up into a loud effect,
and a clipped, truncated or DC-offset master fails with a named finding.
- Loop bounds are authored in samples and re-checked against the *encoded*
rendition, because encoder delay moves a sample-accurate cut.

Sound effects are a distinct capability, not a music cue with a shorter
duration. Until a licence-eligible SFX model is installed, SFX and ambience
generation is refused with that stated reason rather than quietly producing a
two-second orchestral stab from the music model. Importing an owned or licensed
recording puts it through the same finishing and analysis path.

New audio generation has its own switch (`PUBLIC_AUDIO_GENERATION`). Turning it
off stops *new* generation only — the catalog, your saved assets and playback are
untouched, and no accepted asset is ever rewritten.

For the batch path (a whole asset brief, in the background), see
`bun run --cwd apps/backend/image generate:batch`.

## What the game remembers about a saved asset

Saving records more than the bytes. Each saved asset keeps its **generation
lineage** on your device: the engine, the prompt, seed and model id it was
produced with, the transformation chain that prepared the bytes, a content hash
for the raw output and for the prepared artifact, and a scoped rights decision
for each intended use.

Two consequences worth knowing:

- **Reloading is enough.** Close the app, reopen it and open the asset again —
the lineage is still there; it is not rebuilt from guesswork. Assets generated
before this feature simply report their history as *unknown*, which is honest:
the game never invents provenance it did not record.
- **Acceptance binds exact bytes.** A saved asset is *accepted* for local use.
If the underlying prepared bytes change, the old acceptance no longer authorises
the new content and the asset is treated as a new candidate instead. Accepted
content is never silently re-pointed.

The rights record keeps three separate questions apart — may the model be run
(`inference`), may the output ship inside the game (`gameInclusion`), and may it
be redistributed on its own (`standaloneDistribution`). A permission that has not
been resolved is recorded as *unknown* and refuses at the point of publication
rather than being inherited from the model's own licence. Saving an asset locally
is not the same decision as approving it for community sharing, and the game
keeps those separate.

When you publish an asset, the public record carries only the complete shareable
set: the provenance source, engine, prepared content hash and rights states. Your
prompts, model ids, local file paths and private reference art never leave your
device.

## Generated portraits in play

When contextual generation is enabled, the first time you talk to an NPC the game
generates a portrait and registers it as `portraits:<npc>-neutral`. The dialogue
avatar resolver checks that tag before falling back to the catalog sprite, so a
generated portrait wins. Contextual generation is **off by default** and only
runs when you opt in.
