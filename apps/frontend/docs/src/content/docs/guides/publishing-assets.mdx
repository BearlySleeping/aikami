---
title: Publishing and Importing Community Assets
description: How a locally made asset is published to the community namespace, moderated, promoted into the shared content-addressed store, and imported by another player.
---

Aikami's catalog is operator-curated: it is published by CI and lives in a
static, content-addressed index. Community assets are the member-side
counterpart. A creator publishes an asset they made locally, it is staged
**privately**, and nothing becomes public until a moderator approves it.

The publish sequence is deliberately two steps so asset bytes never travel in
a JSON body:

1. **Reserve** — `POST /api/assets/community` validates the metadata,
   category/extension/size and the scoped rights decision, then reserves the
   immutable `(slug, revision)` in D1 with `moderationState: 'pending'` on the
   staging row. A refused publish never uploads a byte.
2. **Upload** — `PUT /api/assets/community/:slug/upload` carries the raw
   `application/octet-stream` body. The hub checks `Content-Length` against
   `MAX_UPLOAD_SIZE` **before** buffering, computes the SHA-256 itself (a
   client-claimed hash is never trusted), and writes the object into the
   private intake bucket (`aikami-uploads`, no public domain).
3. **Moderate** — `POST /api/assets/community/:slug/moderation` moves the row
   to `approved` or `rejected`. Approval *copies* the object into the public
   catalog bucket at `assets/<hash[0:2]>/<hash><ext>` and records
   `promotedAt`; that copy is the only public write on this path, and it is
   idempotent. Rejection leaves the bytes private and records the operator's
   reason.

Because the promoted key is the content hash, the same bytes dedupe across
curated and community content: two members uploading identical bytes share one
object while keeping their own revisions and attribution. Pending bytes are
private at *delivery*, not merely delisted — they live in a bucket with no
public domain and are only readable through an owner-checked route, so knowing
the hash does not grant access.

Discovering and importing is local-first. `GET /api/assets/community` lists
approved, promoted revisions publicly; importing one
(`community_asset_import.ts`) downloads the bytes, re-verifies the advertised
hash, caches them on-device and writes an `r2` source into the local registry.
After that the asset resolves through the ordinary registry/cache path with no
network, exactly like a bundled or generated asset. Importing never silently
replaces a curated tag or a local asset you already accepted — a collision is
surfaced for an explicit decision.

In the Creator Studio, **Publish** on a library entry does both steps for you
and reports the pending state; `/studio/community` browses what others have
published and imports an approved asset into your library with one click. A
publish that the rights gate refuses says so instead of failing silently, and
an import that would shadow a curated tag or an asset you already have asks
before it writes anything.

> **Rights gate, today.** Community distribution is a *scoped* decision, not an
> inherited one: a generated asset does not inherit its model's licence. Until
> C-518 lands the scoped rights record, only an original work with a declared
> shareable licence publishes — a generated asset is refused with
> `rights-unresolved` by design, not by accident. See
> `docs/contracts/C-513-end-user-asset-publishing-and-community-sharing.md`.
